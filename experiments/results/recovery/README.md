# Broker failure and recovery results

Generated by `experiments/recovery/measure_recovery.py` (3 runs per scenario,
500 msg/s, 6 partitions, replication factor 3, `min.insync.replicas=2`).
Raw per-run data: `summary.csv`; 100 ms timelines: `timeline_<run_id>.csv`.

Mean ± standard deviation over 3 runs (seconds unless noted):

| Scenario | Leader failover | Producer stall (all partitions) | Partitions stalled | Worst partition stall | Consumer stall | ISR restored after restart | Lost / duplicate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crash (`kill`), `acks=all` | 8.43 ± 0.83 | 8.27 ± 0.85 | 6 of 6 | 8.82 ± 0.84 | 8.27 ± 0.85 | 2.84 ± 0.10 | 0 / 0 |
| Graceful stop, `acks=all` | 0.16 ± 0.05 | 0.04 ± 0.02 | 0 of 6 | 0.13 ± 0.01 | 0.04 ± 0.02 | 2.58 ± 0.18 | 0 / 0 |
| Crash, `acks=1` | 8.86 ± 0.44 | 0.03 ± 0.00 | 2 of 6 | 10.28 ± 0.58 | 8.65 ± 0.46 | 3.08 ± 0.13 | 0 / 0 |
| Crash of the active controller, `acks=all` | 10.94 ± 0.24 | 10.84 ± 0.22 | 6 of 6 | 11.39 ± 0.19 | 10.84 ± 0.22 | 3.24 ± 0.34 | 0 / 0 |

Observations:

- A crashed broker is only declared dead after its heartbeats stop for
  `broker.session.timeout.ms` (9 s default), so leadership moves after about
  8-9 s. A graceful stop hands leadership over before shutting down (about 0.2 s).
- With `acks=all`, writes to all 6 partitions stall, not only the 2 the dead
  broker led: it is still in every in-sync replica set, so leaders wait for it
  until it is removed.
- With `acks=1`, the producer keeps going on the 4 healthy partitions, but
  consumers still stall: they only see messages up to the high watermark, which
  cannot advance while the dead broker is still in the in-sync set.
- Losing the active controller adds about 2.5 s, because a new KRaft
  controller must be elected before it can move partition leaders.
- No acknowledged message was lost or duplicated in any run.
- The spike in "received" in the first seconds of each plot is the consumer
  catching up after joining its group, not part of the failure.
